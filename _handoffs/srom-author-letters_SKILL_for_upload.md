# SROM letters to authors

Use when Michał asks for an email to an author (or rights holder) about a SROM text: permission to translate, a licence change, a courtesy notice that a translation is under way, a cut, or a follow-up with queries and illustrations. Output: the email(s) ready to paste, in English unless he asks otherwise, then at most three lines of things to check before sending.

## 1. Gather before drafting (silently)

From Redakcja/workspace (STATUS.md, MB-decisions.md, the pool and cards in srom-zizek/volumes/<vol>/, work/<text>/):
- exact title, venue, publisher, year; DOI
- licence of the original, and who holds the rights (author or publisher; check the copyright line)
- what the text does for the block (card § 4–5: what only this text brings, what is new for Polish readers)
- whether it is a late addition, and the real reason it is late
- cuts decided, and what they drop
- Michał's relation to the author (memory /people/; default: formal, first contact)
- the author's rank: Professor (US assistant/associate/full) · Dr (PhD) · Ms/first name if no doctorate. Verify on a faculty page when unsure; otherwise flag it.
- **everything we already hold on each item the letter names** (MB, 26.11.2026): for an image, its srom-ikonograf record in `srom-produkcja/work/<id>/ikonografia/` (the `kontaktowka.json` row; `ikonografia.md` § 2, § 3, § 5 searches, § 6 doubts; `zgody.md`), and the text's notes sheet. Ask only what that leaves open, and say in the letter what we have (source, issue, page, where we saw it). Turda 26.11.2026: a draft asked where the *Sentinela* drawing first appeared and for a better scan; the row already had no. 28, 30.06.1940, p. 7 and the Arcanum scan. Only who drew it and who holds the rights were open.
- **how the text cites each thing you might ask for**: from an archive (archive, fond, call number) or from print (an edition, an anthology, another scholar's book). Ask an author for their own photographs only of what they cite from an archive; what they cite from print they most likely never saw in the original, and we find the print ourselves. Davis 26.11.2026: the decree, the *Opinio*, the conscription data and Rose Foster's letter all came via Heiczinger 1978, Czoernig 1857 and Nelson 2002, so the list was dropped.

## 2. What to ask, by licence

The journal publishes everything under CC BY 4.0 from vol. 19 on.

| Original | Translation allowed? | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| CC BY | yes | nothing: courtesy notice |
| CC BY-NC | yes | consent to CC BY for the translation; if not, it stays CC BY-NC |
| CC BY-NC-ND / ND | no | permission to translate and publish, under CC BY 4.0 |
| closed / © publisher | no | permission, from the rights holder (often the publisher; ask the author to say if so) |

Always: "with full credit and the DOI"; if a publisher may hold rights: "If [Press] must also be asked, please let me know."

## 3. Structure (4–6 short paragraphs, no headings, no bullets in a first letter)

1. **Who we are** (fixed): "I am the managing editor of *Studia Romologica*, a non-commercial, diamond open-access Polish annual of Romani studies." Then the news or the wish in the same paragraph: "We would love to publish…" (permission) / "I am writing to let you know that we are translating…" or "…we are truly delighted to have scheduled the translation of…" (courtesy).
2. **Why this text** (the paragraph that earns the letter). The block sentence, adapted: "The volume's thematic part asks how Roma were caught up in the making of race, and is meant to be another step in our humble quest to move Polish scholarship from an ethnic framing of Roma towards race and related threads." Then one specific, genuine sentence on what *this* text does: name its framework or lens (e.g. Race Before Race; racial capitalism; relational race; the "Zigeuner" as a race denied reason). Praise is warm but hedged ("probably better than anything we have read").
3. **Late addition** (only if true). Give the real, author-specific reason, admitted plainly ("admittedly", "we somehow managed to overlook it initially", "overlap with another paper that frames our block", "we tried hard to find an alternative because it had just appeared in a journal well known to Romani studies scholars"), then why we still could not leave it out. Never a generic "couldn't resist".
4. **The ask** (§ 2), or the cut: say exactly which sections stay and which go, acknowledge what is lost ("an integral part of your insight"), give the reason (no room nor time), end "I hope that is acceptable to you."
5. **Extras, honestly bounded**: other texts we would like to add, with expectations lowered if they may not fit ("given the time constraints and the fact the volume is already barely fitting between the covers, we may not be able to include it").
6. **Illustrations and queries**: "We illustrate our volumes generously" plus a concrete, polite ask with timing ("the process of gathering visual material is already well advanced. I was wondering whether I might trouble you to briefly review the final selection in a few weeks' time, along with a handful of small queries before we go to print."). Omit for theory-heavy texts with no visual programme.
7. **Close**: "I would certainly be glad to send you the volume when it appears." Sign-off: With kind regards, / Michał Bartosz / Managing Editor, *Studia Romologica*.

## 4. Tone (from Michał's own rewrites)

- Warm, courteous, a little effusive and self-deprecating: "truly delighted", "absolutely delightful", "humble quest", "unfortunately", "admittedly", "I was wondering whether I might trouble you". Not clipped or telegraphic; full, flowing sentences with dashes are fine.
- Honest about lateness and limits; explain the reason rather than apologise.
- Specific about the author's work: one real insight, in her or his terms.
- Say "the coming volume", not a volume number or year (dates slip).
- No mention of working drafts or internal translations.
- No promises we may not keep; requests framed as options.
- British spelling. Concise overall: one screen.
- Known contacts (e.g. Ann Ostendorf): first name, informal, shorter, same substance.
- **Once a rapport exists** (a reply or two exchanged, first names): write as Michał talks, not as a report. Lead into a find the way a person would ("I'm not sure you've seen…", "I don't know if you're familiar with…", "A lovely story, but…", "It seems to me just the kind of…"), say why it matters to *their* text in one clause, and let one well-chosen educated word carry it ("a rather wonderful visual gloss on…"). Davis, 22.11.2026, MB: "people don't talk like this" about a bullet that opened with the full German title and ended "the ban on the word reaching even the title of a print".
- **Cut what a reader does not need**: no journal or publisher in a running mention of a work they already have the link to; a famous event by its short name ("the Hont cannibalism trial"), no catalogue detail in the letter (call numbers, page numbers of reports, holders) unless asked; one find, two or three sentences.
- **Claim only what the source shows.** Where a find supports a reading but does not prove it, word it as fitting their argument ("a gloss on the renaming you describe"), not as a fact about intent ("the ban reached the print").

**Feeding a substantive follow-up (MB's rewrite of the Pahulich letter, 22.11.2026).** Talk as one person to another in this thread, with this degree of acquaintance; not a fixed template.
- **Order by attention, not by type.** Open with one line on where things stand ("The translation of your text only needs some touching up, pending your feedback on the below") and signpost the order ("in descending order – starting with the potentially most complex, ending with a few typos…"). First what needs her decision and is interesting (a title to approve; a source problem with a story to it): interest buys attention for the dull rest. Then "Now some lesser questions:". Last, typographic corrections, "just for the record". Trivia up front burns the reader before the part that needs thought.
- **Explain a conundrum as a chain of reasoning**, one step per clause: what the source says → why the existing rendering was fair in its own time and language (concede first) → why carrying it into Polish narrows or shifts it → what we propose → what stays open for her. One example of a kind ("in places harsher: *Nachäffung* is really 'aping'"), not a catalogue of German words.
- **Settle semantics in the author's other language** where she has one: the Polish with the Ukrainian (or hers) beside it („najużyteczniejsi obywatele państwa” / найкорисніші громадяни держави; „poddany/підданий” vs „podmiot/суб’єкт”). Check every form.
- **An aside that invites the scholar** is welcome, hedged as a question ("a curious drift – German cameralist usefulness against a more commercial English idiom, perhaps?"). Fact-check asides like claims (Grellmann was not Prussian: Jena, Göttingen).
- **Say what is negotiable**: what goes in a translator's note regardless, and where we simply take in her wording.
- **Register**: academic, correct, not polished to a comma. Parentheses for asides; a dash to shorten instead of repeating the verb; "we just wanted to clarify", "a minor detail", "we stumbled upon". No bold labels in an ongoing thread; the corrections as one run of semicolons. Editorial "we" for the work, "I" for the personal.
- **When cutting, keep what later lines depend on**: a term introduced once ("the 1807 editor") needs its half-line of definition at first mention. Name the right hand ("Raper's or the 1807 editor's", not "the translator's" for both).
- **Correcting MB's own edit**: keep his order, words and cuts; fix only what is wrong (facts, a broken sentence, a dangling clause, typos) and list each change in one line for him.

## 5. Follow-up letter (after a yes, or mid-thread)

**Read the thread first.** Before a second letter, ask for (or read) the exchange since the first one: the draft must fit where the correspondence now stands, not the plan of weeks ago (Davis 22.11.2026: the old letter 2 thanked them "for agreeing" when they had not yet agreed, asked CC BY consent MB had since waived, and repeated the petition already sent). Do not repeat what an earlier letter said; do not reopen what Michał has settled in his own reply (licence, deadline).
**Once on first-name terms** (they sign with first names): "Dear Sacha and Johanna", sign "Best wishes, Michał", lighter and conversational, still full sentences.
**When the authors have not decided yet** (time, workload): say plainly that nothing in the letter is a condition or needs an answer now, and that it is sent so they can judge the work; most points are already settled on our side.

Then, in order of interest: finds from the illustration research (each with source and link, and the question: did you know it, do you trust it, may we use it); illustration requests (own photographs only of documents cited from an archive, § 1; holder and call number, permission for print and online PDF; sensitive images: first read the holder's own terms on the item page, which often answer "how to handle it", and ask only what they leave open, naming the specific images: whom to consult, warning notes); checks (the few queries that matter most, the full list attached). Bullets are fine here.

**Split letter and attachment.** If MB moves everything into the letter (Pahulich 22.11.2026), mention no attachment; the sheet stays ours. Otherwise, in the letter: at most 3–4 finds and at most 4 checks that touch the argument. Everything else goes in the attachment (`<id>_queries_for_author.md` + `.docx`, pandoc with the Pahulich docx as `--reference-doc`): A. corrected from the records (table: note · as printed · as corrected · record), then one short "in the text, unless you object" list; B. numbered questions, yes/no with the likely answer. MB opens the .docx in the side panel: send it as a file card.
**Licence concerns.** If authors object to CC BY (ND against bad-faith adaptation, back-translation stripping protection, NC against paper mills), do not argue in the letter; record their points in a GEN item for the licence policy and a per-text item for the kolegium exception (knowledge base § Open access and licences).

## 5a. Vetting the queries (before any query goes into a letter)

Lessons from the Pahulich letter (22.11.2026): half the first list was wrong or not worth her time.
- **Re-read the author's own sentence and her bibliography entry** for every query. If her bibliography points to a work (Dal' 1883, vol. VI), search that work, not a same-titled one found first.
- **Test the premise against her wording.** If she writes "nineteenth century", a candidate from the 1760s is out; say what really does not fit ("early" against 1849–1856).
- **One catalogue record does not overrule an author's date.** Check the literature and printed copies first (Przyłuski 1553 was right).
- **Drop** what her cited source already covers and what changes one word (Grellmann's Moscow title, from Willems).
- **Resolve from records whatever can be resolved** (dissertation places from the thesis record): it becomes a correction notice, not a question.
- **Ask yes/no with the likely answer** ("we take this to be X – is that right?"), not open "which/what".
- **Finish half-findings before telling her** ("Kelley is not the editor" → there is no editor; the DOI is a reprint in Brydon's reader).
- **Ask her expertise where it fits** (a Ukrainian author on rendering *Human/Man*).
- **Notices: only what she would see change.** Skip tracking parameters and the like. Write each correction as what changed ("the colon and hyphen in…"), not "X to: Y".
- **Re-check queries inherited from earlier sessions** (RIP lists, ikonografia doubts, old drafts): they were written before the text was read closely. Davis 22.11.2026: of about 25 inherited items, 18 were resolvable from Crossref and catalogues (one a real error: "David" Byrne was Denis), 9 were dropped (the "ages" query contradicted nothing: Nagy's 4–10 under Joseph II matches "as young as four" in 1783; both NT Ordinances, 1911 and 1918, exist), and only 4 checks and 11 short questions were left.
- **Re-read the neighbouring notes when you check a date or name**: a second slip often sits next to the first (note 116 dated the *Opinio* "[1794]" while note 72 had 29 October 1783).
- **Inflection is not misquotation.** "Reaping advantage" for a source's "reap advantage" is not a phantom quotation; claiming "not in that exact form" is the pedantry an author catches at once. The real point is usually context (there: the state making soldiers of them, not landowners or labour). Give each phantom quotation its own nearest passage; do not let two share one.
- **A page outside the cited range** (a chapter on pp. 133–55 cited at p. 107) is a question; a short form with one possible edition is a correction; a short form with several editions (Broome, Goodall) is a one-line question listing the editions.
- **Gender in Polish**: where the author's neutral English ("one Hungarian writer") would force a gender the record does not settle, rephrase neutrally in the Polish (R6) instead of asking.
- **Finds from another session** (ikonografia) go into a letter as that session recorded them; if you could not re-read the source, say so in the note for MB.
- **A source problem under her argument** (a quotation that is the translator's word, not the author's): say what we do and why (fidelity to the original), that her reading holds, list only the words that move, show the proposed translator's note, and invite her to rephrase. Details and pages go in the attachment, not the letter.

## 6. Before handing over

List, in three lines at most: the address to verify; the rank or title if unconfirmed; any fact in the letter that rests on an unverified file entry. Then record in the text's STATUS section that a letter was drafted (not sent).

Below the letter, a short *For MB* note: what was dropped and why (one line each), what was resolved from records instead of asked, what is new. Rewrite the text's RIP notes sheet MB-first in the same pass: "Co po Twojej stronie" (one line per item) → A corrections table → B questions (one line) → C dropped, with reasons → D his calls without the authors → E a technical record of five lines at most; archive the old sheet. The ledger keeps one "send the letter" item per person (R5); when MB says a letter went out, a `korespondencja.tsv` row replaces the item.
