# Register: race, racialisation and their surroundings (srom-tlumacz, leaf 1.3.5)

Started 28.09.2026 19:14 [general]. A reference register for translating EN texts on race and racialisation of Roma
(and neighbouring material: Iberian, Atlantic, early-modern, Polish 17th–19th c.). **Not binding.** Binding forms
are the HOUSE rows of `tlumacz-tb.tsv`; forms harvested here sit there as **CANDIDATE** rows (C-0019–C-0035) or only
here. The first time a text needs one, it goes in that article's queries and MB decides.

Sources read (full text, MB's copies in `training/`, sha256 in `training/manifest.sha256`):
- **taradejna** — P. Taradejna, „Folia Sociologica” 88 (2024), sociology. Useful for vocabulary; weak on facts and
  names (§ F). Its English abstract is a poor translation: never evidence for an EN↔PL pair.
- **nowak** — J. Nowak (IS PAN), „Klio” 72/3 (2024), history of ideas. Careful, well documented; already evidence for
  *urasowienie* (C-0001).

Standing follows the schema: a form in one article is ATTESTED, in both ESTABLISHED. The two-source rule was not
relaxed (MB left the choice to me, 28.09.2026): it measures standing, it does not forbid use; and the weaker of the two
articles is exactly why one source is not enough. Works these articles only cite are marked *via taradejna / via
nowak*: not read, not evidence.

## B. Historical categories, names, etymologies

**Maurowie** (C-0027). Ancient *Mauri* of Mauretania; medieval Muslims of al-Andalus and the Maghreb (mostly
Berbers); early-modern loose use for dark-skinned Africans. Choose by period; the loose sense is queried, never
„Murzyni”. Art and architecture: *styl mauretański* (pl.wikipedia "Maurowie"; a pointer, not evidence).
Siblings likely in Iberian texts (Ostendorf, T19), **not yet checked**: *moriscos* → Moryskowie; *conversos* →
konwertyci (Taradejna uses „konwertyci” generically) or „nowi chrześcijanie”; *marranos* → marrani. Settle with
evidence when a text needs them.

**Murzyn** — not a translation of any EN term we use. EN *Black* → czarny / czarni, lower case (C-0013, MB D11);
*Moor* → Maur. Status: the Rada Języka Polskiego adopted M. Łaziński's opinion on 26.10.2020 (55th plenary):
the word is not to be used „inaczej niż na prawach historycznego cytatu” (rjp.pan.pl, "Opinia o słowach „Murzyn”
i „Murzynka”"). The opinion discusses *czarnoskóry*, *czarny*, and naming by nationality or *Afrykanie*; it
considers *Afropolak* untypical for Polish. Etymology: sources differ (F10): W. Boryś, *Słownik etymologiczny
języka polskiego* — from Old Czech *muřín* / *múřěnín* < Latin *Maurus*, a learned loan from Bible translation;
A. Brückner (1927) — from German *Mohr* < Latin *maurus*; A. Bańkowski doubts the Latin origin (Proto-Slavic *murъ*
'dark'). Said to be attested since the 14th century, from Bible translation (nck.pl; to check in Boryś). Checked: Brückner's entry (Wikisource), RJP
opinion (read); Boryś and Bańkowski only through secondary pages, to be read in the dictionaries.

**czystość krwi** (*limpieza de sangre*, C-0026). Iberian statutes against descendants of converts. Taradejna cites
it from Balibar, as a proto-racist case alongside the Reconquista's ban on mixed marriages.

**Cham, cham, Jafet, Sarmaci** (C-0029). The gentry's genealogy: nobles from Jafet (and from the Sarmatians),
peasants from Cham, cursed; the insult *cham* comes from it. Trepka's *Liber chamorum* (§ D). The Bible curses
Canaan, not Ham: follow what the source says.

**czerń**. WSJP (PAN), sense 'chłopstwo': a 17th-century name for the peasantry of Ukraine, from *czarny* (read
28.09.2026); citations for Cossack commons and Novgorod commoners. Koziołek (Newsweek 2016, via taradejna) adds a
"neutral" sense, peasants of monasteries, from Ruthenian *czerniec* 'monk' (F9). The racial reading, darkness vs
fair skin, is Koziołek's argument. For EN *chern'* / "the rabble" in Cossack contexts: „czerń”, in quotation marks
where the text treats it as a period term. Potocki's line (§ D) is the classic literary use.

**Turańczycy, turański; Ariowie** — Duchiński's thesis of the "Turanian" (non-Slavic, Asian) origin of Russians
(*Zasady dziejów Polski…*, Paris 1858–1861, via nowak). Nowak n. 43: Duchiński put the Turanians in the Asian branch
of the white race; publicists wrote „rasa mongolsko-tatarska”. Studies: Wrzesińska 2015, Górny 2011 (§ G).

**rasa kaukaska / mongolska (żółta) / biała / czarna** — period classification terms. In
translation of period quotations, keep them; in the author's own voice, follow the author (quotation marks if the
author distances).

**Moskal, Moskale** — 19th-c. Polish pejorative for Russians (Nowak: „jak ich pejoratywnie określano”). Only in
quotation or as a quoted period term; never for EN *Russians* / *Muscovites*. EN *Muscovy* / *Muscovites* (early
modern) → Moskwa / Moskwicini? — OPEN, per case (historical place-name policy, MB 27.09.2026).

**horda, „hordy północy”, „dzicz”** — Romantic-era figures of Russians (Mochnacki, Godebski). Period vocabulary.

**przedmurze** (C-0030). Tazbir's formula (via nowak): the antithesis Catholic–pagan becomes civilised European –
barbarian.

***Drang nach Osten*** — Nowak: first in J. Klaczko, *Die deutschen Hegemonen*, Berlin 1849 (her claim, F11).

**chłop pańszczyźniany, pańszczyzna** — serf, serfdom (corvée). EN *serf* → chłop pańszczyźniany; *serfdom* →
pańszczyzna / poddaństwo (poddaństwo = personal subjection; pańszczyzna = labour rent): per passage.

## F. Doubts found (flagged, not corrected; what I would do)

- **F1** taradejna file: the text ends with a note from the tool that converted it ("The document has been formatted
  into Markdown…"): MB's copy is a machine conversion of the PDF. Quotes in the termbase are verified against this
  copy; before a quotation goes to print, check the PDF.
- **F2** taradejna: US stereotype figures misspelt: „Santo” (surely *Sambo*), „Jezebelle” (*Jezebel*); „Welfare Mother”
  (usually *welfare queen*). Would use the standard names; never copy these.
- **F3** taradejna: name slips: „Lewotina” beside „Lewontin”; „Enzo Travesero” in the text, Traverso in the
  bibliography; „Michele'a Foucaulta”; also „zniweolonych”, „biologi”, „perfomatywną”, „ów urasowienie” (owo).
  Some may be conversion errors (F1). No effect on the register; forms taken only from clean passages.
- **F4** taradejna: "in 1997 *American Association of Anthropologists*" appealed to the US government. The 1997
  statement on the OMB race categories came, as far as I know, from the American Anthropological Association;
  not verified here. Irrelevant unless quoted.
- **F5** taradejna: Wielgosz's subtitle differs: note 1 „Jak kapitalizm wynalazł Innych, żeby podporządkować
  wszystkich”, bibliography (and nowak n. 5) „Jak kapitalizm dzieli, by rządzić”. The second is likely right; check
  the title page before citing.
- **F6** taradejna: „Ashenburg 2009” cited (n. 2), not in the bibliography; „Tomala 2023” in the bibliography, not
  cited; Foucault, *Porządek dyskursu* dated 1970 with a Polish publisher (the lecture was given in 1970; the Polish
  edition is later). Check before using any of these references.
- **F7** taradejna: Cartwright's 1851 "disease" is *drapetomania*; the article gives only „uciekinierstwo”. Would
  keep *drapetomania* with a gloss.
- **F8** taradejna: „samooznaczanie” as Goffman's term: I cannot connect it to *Stigma*; check the Polish edition
  before using it as Goffman's.
- **F9** czerń: taradejna (< Koziołek, a Newsweek piece) derives the neutral sense from *czerniec* 'monk'; WSJP
  derives *czerń* 'chłopstwo' from *czarny*. The two may fit (the "black" clergy), but the monastic sense has no
  scholarly source in hand. Would follow WSJP.
- **F10** Murzyn: pl.wikipedia "Maurowie" gives German *Mohr* (= Brückner 1927); pl.wikipedia "Murzyn" gives Old
  Czech *muřín* (= Boryś); Bańkowski dissents. Would cite Boryś as the current standard and name the alternatives;
  never cite Wikipedia.
- **F11** nowak: *Drang nach Osten* first in Klaczko 1849: her claim, not checked.
- **F12** nowak: n. 28 „Demokrata Polska” (elsewhere „Demokrata Polski”); n. 25 title „Otwarcie kursu historii
  statystyki Polski” vs text „kursu historii i statystyki”; Mochnacki quotation „jakącą się” (garbled, probably
  „jaka się”; F1 applies to this copy too). Check the PDF before quoting.
- **F13** nowak n. 17: Górny's article in „Kwartalnik Historyczny” 2011 given as „t. 68”; the journal's 2011
  volume should be around 118. To check when the article is sourced (G5).
