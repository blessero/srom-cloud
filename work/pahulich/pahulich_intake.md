## 4. Doubts in the source (flagged, not corrected; what I would do)

1. "the Austro-Hungarian sovereigns Maria Theresa and Joseph II" — Austria-Hungary dates from 1867: anachronism. Translated as written; I would propose „habsburscy władcy” to the author.
2. "in Royal Habsburg Hungary in the fifteenth century" — Royal Hungary (Habsburg) exists only from 1526. As written; question to the author (source: Crowe 2007, s. 72–73).
3. "Only in the early nineteenth century, Moldavia's ruler Ghica outlawed the separation of enslaved Romani children from their parents" — which Ghica? Grigore III Ghica (Moldavia, 1764–1767, 1774–1777) is usually credited with a 1766 ban on separating families; the early nineteenth century does not fit a Moldavian Ghica (Grigore Alexandru Ghica ruled 1849–1856). As written; check Crowe 2007, s. 118 (not at hand), question to the author.
4. Dal' "in 1848": the quoted passage not found in «Цыганка» (1830); the text behind "Dal 1883, t. 6, s. 140–142" not identified. Ask the author for the title of the text.
5. Przyłuski's *Leges*: BN dates the edition 1551 (printing completed 1553 per some bibliographies — not verified). As written.
6. Münster, *Cosmographiae Universalis* "(1544)": 1544 is the German *Cosmographia*; the Latin *Cosmographiae universalis libri VI* is 1550. As written; minor.
7. Grellmann (1807, p. 108) "for the purpose of civilising… scarcely deserving to be considered as human beings": the sentence looks like the 1807 English editor's, not Grellmann's (see § 1). The author attributes it to Grellmann. Query to MB (and maybe the author).
8. "as explained in note 1" (n. 67): now note 2 (conversion) — updated in the translation, with a comment.
9. Note 128 "Germany was the cradle of violent anti-Roma laws." — an unsupported sweeping claim in a note; translated as written, no action.
10. From D17 (srom-typeset): n. 37 „(1992, 81)”, "Jenkins and Leroy (2021)" — kept, comments carried.
