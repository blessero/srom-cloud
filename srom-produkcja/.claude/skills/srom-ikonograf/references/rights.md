# Rights, credits, permissions

SROM is wholly non-commercial (grant-funded, copies given away free, open access paid by the publisher) and an
academic journal: most holders grant permission readily, often free. Journal facts for letters (publisher, ISSN,
licence of the articles): srom-kanon `references/SROM_knowledge_base.md`, never from memory. What the caption prints
for each class (the metryka's rights element): Kanon § 10.3, point 5; this file decides the class, the Kanon the words.

## Classes (one per candidate, on the contact sheet and in the record)

| class | what | use | rights element (Kanon § 10.3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** free | public domain, PDM, CC0, "No known restrictions" (LoC) | use | none; holder and shelfmark still in the source |
| **B** attribution | CC BY, CC BY-SA, Fortepan, Bundesarchiv on Commons | use; credit as the licence asks | licence code + version; the licensor when not the maker |
| **C** non-commercial / no-derivatives | CC BY-NC(-SA), CC BY-NC-ND (David Rumsey, many regional archives on Europeana) | NC: fits SROM. **ND: no crop, detail or retouch** — MB often cuts maps and details, so flag ND before he plans a crop | as B |
| **D** ask | in copyright or reuse reserved, holder known (IPN, museums, agencies, living photographers, authors' archives) | letter (below); usable once granted | `Repr. za zgodą <holder>.` or the holder's wording |
| **E** unknown | author and holder unknown or unreachable (user uploads on fotopolska.eu and similar) | MB decides per image, after due diligence | none (`fot. autor nieznany` is the maker element); the due diligence stays in the record |
| **F** paid | Alamy, Look and Learn, agencies | last resort, MB approves the fee | the agency's credit line |

The author's own photographs and collections: the article licence covers them unless the author says otherwise; no
rights element. An author's photograph of someone else's object (an archival record, a museum piece) is two things:
the photograph is the author's, the object's holder may still set terms for publishing it (ask, rights.md § Letters).

Notes:
- **Reproductions of public-domain art.** Under art. 14 of the EU DSM directive (in force in Poland and Spain), a faithful
  photograph or scan of a public-domain visual work is not itself protected; a holder's "all rights reserved" on such
  a scan does not make it class D. Credit the holder still. Photographs of three-dimensional objects can be protected
  as photographs: treat them by the photographer's terms.
- **"pdus" (HathiTrust) and US-only public domain** (US works before 1930, US federal works): fine for a US holder's
  scan of a pre-1900 item; for 20th-century items check the author's death date (EU: 70 years).
- **The article's licence vs the image's.** The image keeps its own terms; the article's CC BY does not extend to
  it. Settled in Kanon § 13.2 (v1.18): the metryka states the terms; nothing more, per caption or on the first page.
- **Lead times.** Museums and archives: 1–4 weeks, longer in summer; IPN and ministries: longer. Ask at the brief
  stage, not after layout.

## Due diligence for class E (record it in § 4)

1. Reverse image search and Tier A search for another, attributed copy (sources.md § Tracing).
2. Ask the uploader or site: who took it, where it comes from, do they claim rights (the fotopolska case: uploader did
   not know the author and made no claim).
3. Record date, whom you asked, the answer; then the question to MB: use with `Fot. autor nieznany`, or drop.

## Finding the contact (step 7, chosen images only)

The aim is a person who can say yes, not a general inbox. Per holder, in this order, and stop when one is good:
1. The holder's own **reproduction / rights / image licensing** page (often "Reproductions", "Image requests",
   "Rights and reproductions", "Bildrechte", "Droits de reproduction", "Udostępnianie zbiorów"): a named officer, an
   address, or a request form. A form counts: give MB its URL and the fields to fill instead of a letter.
2. The department that holds the item (prints and drawings, photographs, manuscripts, the archive's photo collection):
   its curator or head on the staff page, with e-mail.
3. The item page itself: a "contact us about this item" link, the photographer's or donor's name for a living
   photographer, the agency for an agency photo.
4. A general address (info@, archive@) as the fallback, said so.
Give **one recommended contact and up to two alternatives** when unsure (a curator and the reproductions office,
say), each with name, role, e-mail and the page where it was found, so MB can check it. Never guess an address from a
pattern (firstname.lastname@): mark one not seen on a page as "not found". For a living photographer or an author,
the person; for a Commons file, the holder or photographer it came from, not the uploader (unless the uploader is the
photographer). A holder already asked for another text or image in the volume: one letter, or a note in the earlier one.

## `zgody.md` (one per text, in `work/<id>/ikonografia/`)

One block per holder, ready to send: **To** (recommended contact; alternatives below it), **Subject**, the letter
(EN template below, all brackets filled), then for the record: where each contact was found, the holder's stated
terms or fee if published, and the lead time. The record's § 4 points to it (`🔴 zgody.md`) and keeps the state.

## Letters

Fill the brackets; one letter per holder listing all its items. **English by default** (MB, 24.10.2026); PL/ES/FR
only when MB asks. Per item: title as catalogued, creator, date, holder's shelfmark or inventory number, the item
URL, and the intended use (print, about [⅓/½/full] page, [b/w or colour]; the online PDF). Ask for the best file and
the preferred credit line. Send from MB's address; record the date in § 4. MB's title in the PL/ES/FR letters (sekretarz redakcji / secretario de redacción /
secrétaire de rédaction) is a draft for "managing editor": MB corrects it once, here. Print run: not in the knowledge
base; the letter does without it unless a holder asks.

**Author (EN; in Polish to a Polish author)** — at the brief stage (SKILL.md step 1)
> Subject: Images for your article in *Studia Romologica* [vol.]
>
> Dear [name],
>
> We illustrate the articles in *Studia Romologica* with period sources and photographs. For "[title]", do you have
> your own photographs of the archival documents you cite (e.g. [one or two from the notes]), field photographs, or
> images you would suggest? For each image, please tell us who made it and when, where the original is kept (the
> archive, library or museum, and its call number or inventory number), and whether we may reproduce it in print and
> in the online PDF; for a photograph you took of a document, also whether the archive allowed publication. We will
> credit each as you wish.
>
> With kind regards, Michał Bartosz, Managing Editor, *Studia Romologica*
