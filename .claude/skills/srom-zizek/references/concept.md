# Concept — sharpening the idea the block is built around

MB usually arrives with a good idea of the block. This pass does not replace it; it tests it, widens it
where widening pays, and finds what it cannot see. Output goes into `volumes/<vol>/concept.md` under a
dated heading; the conversation happens in chat. Can be run at any stage — before the pool exists, or
again when the candidates start pulling the idea somewhere else.

## Steps

1. **Restate it at its strongest.** The idea in 3–5 sentences as a research question the block answers,
   plus the claims it quietly assumes (e.g. "that Roma were racialized before modern race science").
   Ask MB to correct the restatement before going on — a wrong restatement poisons everything after it.

2. **Strands.** Split the idea into 3–5 strands a block could carry (period, region, method, scale,
   genre of text). Mark which strands the current pool covers and which are empty.

3. **Lenses — triangulation.** Read the idea through each lens; 3–6 bullets per lens, each a concrete
   observation, question or reading suggestion. Claims about the literature need a retrieved source.
   - *Historian*: periodisation, archives, what the sources can and cannot bear.
   - *Anthropologist*: lived Romani realities vs discourse about Roma; fieldwork evidence.
   - *Theorist*: the conceptual apparatus (race, colonialism, modernity, whiteness…) — does it fit Roma and CEE, or strain?
   - *Romani perspective*: Romani scholars, activists, testimonies; epistemic justice — who speaks in this block.
   - *Polish field*: how it lands in Polish discourse (`context/polish-field.md`), what Polish work it answers or ignores.
   - *Skeptic*: the strongest case against the whole concept — where it is fashionable but hollow, or already done.
   Default: all lenses inline in one session. For a big new theme MB may ask for *deep concept research*:
   one subagent per lens, each with search, each returning ≤400 words with sources; then continue here.

4. **Tensions.** Where the lenses contradict each other. These are not problems to hide: the best blocks
   stage one or two of them (paper A against paper B). Name the tension and what would settle it.

5. **Blind spots.** What none of MB's framing and none of the lenses touched. Check the archive too: has SROM
   done this before (`context/srom-archive.md`; grep `context/srom-abstracts.tsv` for the argument's terms)? What would a Romani reader, a Polish historian, a critic of
   the theory each say is missing?

6. **Expansions.** Adjacent literatures, authors and specific texts worth reading or soliciting — verified,
   with links, marked by how they would enter: as candidate / as background for the editorial intro / as a
   name to invite.

7. **Questions for MB.** 3–5, the ones whose answers would change the block most.

## Rules
- Push back. If the idea is weaker than MB thinks, say so and say why; if it is stronger, say that too.
- Keep MB's lines of thought distinguishable from Zizek's additions — label additions [Z].
- Distinguish "the literature shows" (sourced) from "I suspect" (unsourced, flagged).
